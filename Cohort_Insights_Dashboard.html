Finding 1 · The starting line

A genuinely mixed-ability room.

The cohort splits almost evenly between confident users and near-beginners. Seven people use AI regularly; seven have only tried it once or twice; three have never touched it. You'll need to hold both ends — enough depth for the regulars, enough patience for the first-timers.

AI experience level

Self-reported, all 21 registrants

What this means for pacing

The "tried once or twice" group (7) is your sweet spot — curious, low confidence, high upside. Design the live demos for them.

The regulars · 7

Already use AI in work or daily life. Keep them engaged with the agentic AI and advanced prompting moments.

The beginners · 3

Never used any tool. The account-setup section and a buddy seat next to a confident user will carry them.

Finding 3 · What they already use

ChatGPT leads. Claude is wide open.

The cohort's existing tool familiarity shapes your teaching. ChatGPT and WhatsApp's Meta AI dominate; Claude — your primary teaching tool — has been touched by only 3 people. That's an opportunity, not a problem: most of the room will meet Claude fresh, exactly as you intend.

Three things to plan around

Familiar ground

16 use ChatGPT, 13 use WhatsApp Meta AI. Anchor early examples in what they know, then show why Claude shines for the work they care about.

Fresh ground

Only 3 have used Claude. No bad habits to unlearn — you're teaching the formula on a clean slate for most of the room.

Watch for

Grammarly (11) & Copilot (10) are common. Some arrive thinking "AI = spellcheck." Part of the job is widening that picture.

Finding 4 · The setup in the room

Plan for every screen size.

Fifteen will bring a Windows laptop — good for hands-on work. But seven are coming smartphone-only, and seven bringing tablets. Your account-setup section and live exercises need to work across all three. And with 81% non-coders, the non-technical framing of the whole masterclass is firmly validated by the data.

Coding experience: the framing is right

17 of 21 (81%) are not coders or developers. One writes code; one has dabbled. This confirms the masterclass should stay firmly non-technical — but the HTML/website-build demo will still land as genuinely impressive precisely because almost nobody in the room writes code.

Finding 5 · The professional mix

A boardroom's worth of range.

The cohort spans finance, ministry, law, healthcare, oil & gas, education, HR, IT and more. This breadth is why the use-case library was built across so many categories — and why the eight live demos deliberately map to different professions in the room.

Finance and oil & gas lead the industry mix, with a strong professional-services presence (law, accounting, HR). Several registrants named multiple sectors — e.g. "Ministry and Textile," "Oil & Gas and Agriculture" — reflecting how many wear more than one hat.
